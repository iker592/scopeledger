# A reply to “can you just add this?”

You can be helpful and still make the decision explicit. The most useful response offers the client something concrete to approve.

Use this sequence: acknowledge the desired result, refer to the current deliverable, describe the addition, and offer a price-and-time decision.

Paid addition:

“Happy to add [requested result]. The current scope covers [existing deliverable]. This adds [specific new work]. The additional fee is [amount], and delivery moves from [date] to [date]. I’ll send a short change order so you can confirm before I start.”

Swap to stay within budget:

“We can keep the current budget by replacing [existing item] with [new item]. That would mean [clear consequence]. If you prefer to keep both, the added fee is [amount] and the delivery date would be [date]. Which option fits your priorities?”

Defer until after launch:

“This can be phase two. We can finish the currently agreed launch scope first and estimate [new item] separately. That keeps the current delivery plan clear.”

Recorded courtesy:

“I can include [specific item] as a courtesy on this project. Its usual estimate is [amount/hours], which I’m waiving here. The remaining agreed scope and review rounds remain as described.”

Ambiguous original scope:

“Our original description leaves this detail unclear. Let’s agree on the exact result before I continue. Here is what I understood to be included: [details]. Here is the additional work needed for your request: [details].”

Do not send an estimate as though it is an approved invoice. Put a start condition in the message, and retain the response. If a client declines, discuss priorities rather than quietly completing unpaid work and arguing afterward.

Research context: a recent [website freelancer discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/website/comments/1wztmii/freelance_website_scope_creep_is_eating_my/) describes difficulty communicating revision limits and extra requests. These scripts are original examples addressing that problem; they are not quoted client communications.
